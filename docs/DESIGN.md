# Game design

The fantasy: enormous caverns underground, with whole forests, lakes and seas in them. The
current stage is a proof of concept, in which you walk and fly around a generated world and
break and place blocks, as in Minecraft's creative mode. Each decision below records who made
it. "Player" means it was chosen during design consultation; "engineering" means it was left to
implementation and is open to change.

## World structure

**Layers.** The world is three stacked layers, 1,200 blocks apart, sealed in rock. Bedrock caps
both ends at heights 768 and -2816. It's flat on the void side and jagged on the inside, and can't
be broken. Past it there's only empty void.

**Caverns.** Within a layer, each column has a floor height and a ceiling height. A cavern is
wherever the ceiling is above the floor. Those two surfaces come from broad noise, which is
what guarantees wide, walkable floors. Pure 3D noise makes Swiss cheese with few flat areas.
3D noise then roughens walls and adds overhangs, on top of that shape.

**Plains.** The Overworld and Dark Forest alternate between rolling hills and plains, in regions
several hundred blocks across. A plain is a series of level shelves joined by gentle ramps, with
one shelf just above the water so that shores stay dry. Ground gets rougher again before a mountain
starts to rise. The Underworld has no plains.

**Features inside a cavern**

- *Rock pillars*: hourglass columns joining floor and ceiling, usually 22-82 blocks in radius.
- *Stalactites and stalagmites*: rock spikes, rare in the Overworld and common in the Underworld.
- *Chasms*: rare vertical shafts of 28-90 blocks in radius, connecting a layer to the next.
  They only open on dry ground, because static water couldn't pour down them.

**Liquids.** Everything open below a layer's liquid level is water, or lava in the Underworld, so
one plane spans the whole layer. Terrain height also drifts by +/-150 blocks over a few
kilometres, so one layer holds both dry land and open lakes or seas.

### Overworld

The cozy starting place. Crystal covers nearly the whole ceiling in soft blue, so it reads as
sky, with blue haze in the distance. Round broadleaf trees grow in stands with meadows between
them, under rare giants that serve as landmarks. Sand rims the lakes and seas.

### Dark Forest

Dim and foggy. Dark grass lies under a very dense forest of conifers, with giant ones rising out
of it. Scattered mountains of bare slate stand clear of the trees, which never grow on them. Only
a few pale crystal fields light it. Small ponds are rimmed with gravel.

### Underworld

No plants at all. Black basalt mountains rise from fields of ash around lakes of churning lava,
which lights everything from below and fills the air with red haze. Glowing veins run across the
mountains, and a few embers glow in the ceiling.

## Known simplifications at this stage

- No inventory screen, no creatures, no health or fall damage. The hotbar only fills by picking
  blocks from the world. Sneaking doesn't lower the camera, and there's no auto-jump.
- Crystal and lava don't cast local light. They look like they do because the ambient field
  follows them at large scale, but a single colony you carve free or place won't brighten what's
  nearby.
- Edits exist only at full detail: chunks beyond 384 blocks show the unedited world, so placed
  blocks vanish there too. Edits also aren't saved to disk, and R discards them.
- Breaking a block below the water line leaves a hole of air beside a standing wall of water,
  and lava behaves the same. Swimming in lava does no harm, since there's no health.

## Ideas for later

Flowing water and lava; tunnels joining caverns horizontally; local light from crystal and lava;
ground plants; biomes within a layer; an inventory screen; auto-jump.
